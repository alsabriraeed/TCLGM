## 1. Mathematical Formulation (Preview-Ready Markdown)

Because outcomes for unobserved paths are unavailable at recommendation time, TCLGM estimates prospective learning gains using a recurrent knowledge-tracing cognitive simulator based on Deep Knowledge Tracing (DKT).

### 1.1 Cognitive State Transition & Concept Mastery

Following the canonical DKT formulation, each interaction $(q_n, a_n)$ of concept $q_n \in \{0, \dots, K-1\}$ and binary response $a_n \in \{0, 1\}$ is mapped into a joint interaction embedding:

$$
x_n^{\mathrm{KT}} = E_{\mathrm{inter}}(q_n + K \cdot a_n) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{emb}}}
$$

where $E_{\mathrm{inter}} \in \mathbb{R}^{2K \times d_{\mathrm{emb}}}$ is a trainable interaction embedding lookup table.

An LSTM network recurrently updates the hidden cognitive state $g_{u,n} = (h_{u,n}, c_{u,n})$:

$$
(h_{u,n}, c_{u,n}) = \operatorname{LSTM}\left(x_n^{\mathrm{KT}}, \; (h_{u,n-1}, c_{u,n-1})\right)
$$

The learner's concept mastery vector $m_u^n \in [0, 1]^K$ is projected from the hidden state $h_{u,n} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{KT}}}$ via a linear readout layer followed by a sigmoid activation:

$$
m_u^n = \operatorname{sigmoid}\left(W_m h_{u,n} + b_m\right)
$$

where $W_m \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times d_{\mathrm{KT}}}$ and $b_m \in \mathbb{R}^K$ are trainable parameters. The component $m_{u,j}^n$ represents the predicted probability that learner $u$ correctly answers a future assessment associated with concept $c_j$. The initial recommendation-time cognitive state $g_u^0$ and mastery vector $m_u^0$ are obtained by unrolling the model over the learner's complete observed history $H_u$.

---

### 1.2 Recommendation Rollout Simulation

When evaluating a prospective study path $p = (p_1, \dots, p_L)$ generated by the planner, the simulator updates the cognitive state step-by-step. At rollout step $t$:

1. The response is simulated based on current mastery:
   $$
   \widetilde{a}_t \sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}\left(m_{u, p_t}^{t-1}\right)
   $$
2. The cognitive state advances by observing the simulated interaction:
   $$
   (h_u^t, c_u^t) = \operatorname{LSTM}\left(E_{\mathrm{inter}}(p_t + K \cdot \widetilde{a}_t), \; (h_u^{t-1}, c_u^{t-1})\right)
   $$
3. The updated mastery vector is derived:
   $$
   m_u^t = \operatorname{sigmoid}\left(W_m h_u^t + b_m\right)
   $$

The simulator supports both:
* **Stochastic rollouts (`simulate_path_detailed`):** Sampling $\widetilde{a}_t \sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}(m_{u, p_t}^{t-1})$ with repeated attempts upon failure, averaged over $R$ rollouts to yield expected learning gains.
* **Deterministic single-pass study (`simulate_path_src_style`):** Sequentially setting $\widetilde{a}_t = 1$ for all concepts in the recommended curriculum, matching standard curriculum evaluation protocols during policy training.

---

### 1.3 Simulator Training Objective

The cognitive simulator is trained end-to-end on historical learner interaction logs using the next-response binary cross-entropy loss:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KT}} = -\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{KT}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{KT}}} \left[ a_i \log \widehat{a}_i + (1 - a_i) \log (1 - \widehat{a}_i) \right]
$$

where $N_{\mathrm{KT}}$ is the total number of transition pairs across all learner sequences, $a_i \in \{0, 1\}$ is the observed ground-truth response at step $n+1$, and $\widehat{a}_i = m_{u_i, q_{n+1}}^n$ is the predicted probability for the target concept.

## 3. Discrepancy & Code Verification Analysis

1. **Joint Interaction Embedding vs. Separate Concept/Response Encoders:**
   * *Draft:* $g_{u,n} = \operatorname{KT}(g_{u,n-1}, E_q(q_n), E_a(a_n))$ with separate encoders $E_q$ and $E_a$.
   * *Code:* [`models/dkt.py:34, 66–67`](file:///home/raed/TCLGM/models/dkt.py#L34-L67) uses the canonical DKT joint embedding table `Embedding(2 * num_c, emb_size)` indexed by $q_n + K \cdot a_n$.
2. **Recurrent Network Type:**
   * *Draft:* Left abstract as $\operatorname{KT}$.
   * *Code:* [`models/dkt.py:36`](file:///home/raed/TCLGM/models/dkt.py#L36) explicitly uses an **LSTM** (`nn.LSTM`).
3. **Auxiliary Duration Head ($f_T$) — Major Mismatch:**
   * *Draft:* Stated that an auxiliary network $f_T(g_{u,n}, e_{q_{n+1}})$ predicts log-durations to regularize cognitive representations.
   * *Code:* [`training/train_simulator.py:73–93`](file:///home/raed/TCLGM/training/train_simulator.py#L73-L93) trains the simulator **exclusively** with the next-response binary cross-entropy loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KT}}$. There is **no auxiliary duration loss** during simulator training. Time modeling is entirely decoupled into Step 2 (`PersonalizedTimeCostModel`).
   * *Clarification on Auxiliary Heads in TCLGM:* There is a `KTAuxHead` in the codebase ([`models/kt_aux_head.py`](file:///home/raed/TCLGM/models/kt_aux_head.py)), but it acts on the **path decoder policy** in Step 4 ([`training/constrained_optimization.py:172–185`](file:///home/raed/TCLGM/training/constrained_optimization.py#L172-L185)), regularizing the decoder states to predict concept mastery, not durations.
4. **Simulator Training Loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KT}}$:**
   * *Draft:* Binary cross-entropy next-response loss.
   * *Code:* [`training/train_simulator.py:86`](file:///home/raed/TCLGM/training/train_simulator.py#L86) uses `F.binary_cross_entropy(pred_clamped, target_valid)`, which matches Eq.~\eqref{eq:ktloss} with 100% precision.

\subsubsection{Cognitive State-Transition Simulation}

Because outcomes for unobserved paths are unavailable at recommendation time, TCLGM estimates prospective learning gains using a knowledge-tracing cognitive simulator based on Deep Knowledge Tracing (DKT) \cite{piech2015dkt}. Each interaction $(q_n, a_n)$ of concept $q_n \in \{0, \ldots, K-1\}$ and binary correctness $a_n \in \{0, 1\}$ is represented via a joint interaction embedding:
\begin{equation}
x_n^{\mathrm{KT}} = E_{\mathrm{inter}}(q_n + K \cdot a_n),
\label{eq:interemb}
\end{equation}
where $E_{\mathrm{inter}} \in \mathbb{R}^{2K \times d_{\mathrm{KT}}}$ is a trainable lookup matrix. An LSTM network recurrently updates the hidden cognitive state $g_{u,n} = (h_{u,n}, c_{u,n})$:
\begin{equation}
(h_{u,n}, c_{u,n}) = \operatorname{LSTM}\left( x_n^{\mathrm{KT}}, \, (h_{u,n-1}, c_{u,n-1}) \right).
\label{eq:simulatorstate}
\end{equation}
The learner's concept mastery vector $m_u^n \in [0, 1]^K$ is projected from the hidden state $h_{u,n} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{KT}}}$:
\begin{equation}
m_u^n = \operatorname{sigmoid}\left( W_m h_{u,n} + b_m \right),
\label{eq:masteryhead}
\end{equation}
where $W_m \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times d_{\mathrm{KT}}}$ and $b_m \in \mathbb{R}^K$ are trainable parameters. The component $m_{u,j}^n$ represents the predicted probability that learner $u$ correctly answers an item associated with concept $c_j$. The initial recommendation-time cognitive state $g_u^0$ and mastery vector $m_u^0$ are obtained by unrolling the model over the learner's complete observed history $H_u$.

For a concept $p_t$ recommended at path step $t$, the simulator samples a response and advances the cognitive state:
\begin{equation}
\widetilde a_t \sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}\left( m_{u,p_t}^{t-1} \right), \qquad (h_u^t, c_u^t) = \operatorname{LSTM}\left( E_{\mathrm{inter}}(p_t + K \cdot \widetilde a_t), \, (h_u^{t-1}, c_u^{t-1}) \right),
\label{eq:rollouttransition}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
m_u^t = \operatorname{sigmoid}\left( W_m h_u^t + b_m \right),
\label{eq:rolloutmastery}
\end{equation}
where $\widetilde a_t \in \{0, 1\}$ is the simulated response. Performing repeated rollouts produces a distribution over the mastery trajectory $m_u^{(0:L)} = (m_u^0, \ldots, m_u^L)$, avoiding the unrealistic assumption that every recommended interaction is successfully mastered.

The simulator is trained on historical interaction sequences using the next-response binary cross-entropy loss:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KT}} = -\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{KT}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{KT}}} \left[ a_i \log \widehat a_i + (1 - a_i) \log(1 - \widehat a_i) \right],
\label{eq:ktloss}
\end{equation}
where $N_{\mathrm{KT}}$ is the total number of transition pairs, $a_i \in \{0, 1\}$ is the observed response, and $\widehat a_i$ is its predicted probability. Prospective time costs for candidate admission and budget evaluation are supplied by the dedicated personalized time-cost model in Eq.~\eqref{eq:expectedcost}, keeping cognitive state dynamics cleanly decoupled from duration modeling.
